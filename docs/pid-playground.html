Bonus A — Ziegler–Nichols ultimate-gain experiment

The original first-order and positively damped second-order plants cannot produce a finite P-only ultimate gain in the ideal continuous model. This separate teaching plant uses G(s) = 1/(s+1)³ with unity negative feedback. Its theoretical ultimate gain is Ku = 8 and ultimate period is Pu = 2π/√3 ≈ 3.628 s. The original PID plants remain unchanged.

For this plant, the characteristic equation is (s+1)³ + Kp = 0. At marginal stability, Kp = 8 and ω = √3 rad/s. The measured period is estimated from late positive peaks. Sustained oscillation is a property of this bonus plant, not of the original two PID plants.

What P, I and D do

P: Usually speeds up the response, but often leaves a steady-state error. I: Removes step steady-state error for a stable, unsaturated loop, but can cause overshoot. D: Adds damping in many systems, often reducing overshoot; its effect depends on the plant and tuning.
