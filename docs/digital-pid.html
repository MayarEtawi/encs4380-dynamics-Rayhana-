QUICK REFERENCE

Understand each update.

01 Error

Measure the output and subtract it from the target at the current sample.

e[k] = r[k] − y[k]
First sample: previous error and integral are zero.

02 Digital PID

Combine current error, accumulated error and error change.

P[k] = Kp · e[k]
I[k] = I[k−1] + Ki · Ts · e[k]
D[k] = Kd · e[k] − e[k−1]Ts
Ki includes no hidden time scaling; Ts is in seconds.

03 Limit & hold

Clamp P + I + D to the actuator limits. Keep this command until the next sample.

u[k] = clip(P + I + D)
Anti-windup can reject the integral increment. It cannot make an unreachable target reachable.
Numerical method, metrics & implementation assumptions

The first-order and mass–spring–damper models follow Homework 1 Modules 1 and 3. Only the physical plant is integrated by RK4. Integration steps are no larger than 0.005 s and one fortieth of the plant characteristic time, and end exactly at controller samples. The command is held between samples. Changing Ts changes the controller, rather than reducing solver accuracy.

The sum includes the current sample error. The derivative is a backward difference of error, as specified in the homework; it can produce a kick on a setpoint change. Conditional anti-windup rejects an integral increment when the candidate command is outside a limit and that increment pushes further into the limit. An increment that helps recovery is allowed.

Overshoot is relative to the target. Rise time is the first 10–90% upward crossing of the target. Settling uses a ±2% target band and requires at least one final second in that band. Final tracking error uses the mean of the final second; it is called steady-state error only if the tail range is ≤0.2% of the target. Unsettled and divergent runs are labelled explicitly. A recovery step is applied at the first controller sample at or after its requested time; metrics then refer to this second step, and rise time is not reported.

Arduino uses micros(), non-blocking scheduling, seconds for Ts, the same integral update and initial conditions, and a held actuator output. A missed deadline is resynchronised rather than replayed using stale measurements. Sensor calibration, driver wiring and PWM quantisation depend on the real project and are not simulated. The sensor/driver functions must be connected before hardware use.

Project application: select the first-order plant, enter measured K and τ from My System, use speed units for the setpoint and 0–100% for PWM limits, tune the controller, then copy the settings link and Arduino code. Neither the rated motor speed nor a teaching preset establishes the motor’s time constant.
